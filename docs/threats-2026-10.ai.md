# Security threats as of October 2026

> **This is a dated snapshot, not a live feed.** It was put together on October 22, 2026 from a few days of research and checked against the sources linked here. Threats change every week. Before you act on anything below, have Claude or another assistant look up the current advisories for what you use, and do more thorough research for anything that matters to you.

## The short version

- **AI is speeding up both sides.** Researchers used AI models to find serious flaws in PHP, the software behind most websites. One attacker used AI agents to break into more than 100 company websites for about $50 each. Expect faster attacks after every new flaw, and more convincing scams.
- **Attacks are automated, so ordinary people and small sites get hit.** Tools now scan the whole internet for anything unpatched, from small business websites to home media servers.
- **Poisoned software is a main way in.** Attackers slip malware into trusted packages, plugins, and even hardware sold by resellers. Some of it fights back when you try to remove it.
- **Stolen keys turn straight into money.** AI service keys stolen from one coding tool were used to run up hundreds of dollars in charges overnight.
- **The official vulnerability database is behind.** Vendor advisories and the US government's list of actively exploited flaws are faster.

## This week (October 15 to 21)

- **PHP fixes two critical flaws (Oct 21).** The two flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, are rated 9.2 out of 10. They let a stranger take over a web server without logging in. Fixed versions are 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35, and PHP 8.1 and older get no new releases, so upgrade or ask your host for a patched build. Researchers at Calif and Wiz say they used AI models to find them. Government services in several European countries, including the Netherlands, and some companies took sites offline as a precaution while they patched. Amazon reports attack attempts since October 17; no break-in had been confirmed at release. Updating WordPress or its plugins does not fix this: your web host has to update PHP. [php.net](https://www.php.net/archive/2026.php#2026-10-21-5), [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/php2shell-cve-2026-109618-cve-2026-109619-frequently-asked-questions-about-critical-php-remote)
- **One attacker, many AI agents (Reuters, Oct 19).** Since September 25, a single Chinese-speaking attacker let AI agents try more than 1,200 websites in the US, Canada, Australia, and Japan, and broke into sites belonging to more than 100 companies. The researcher who found it, at the security firm NorForge, estimates about $50 and 23 hours per break-in. Reuters could not establish how much data, if any, was taken. Reuters describes the tools as a mix of DeepSeek and Claude Code; NorForge reads its evidence, a single label, as Claude Code running a DeepSeek model. [Reuters, via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/19/how-one-hacker-used-ai-agents-and-a-few-thousand-dollars-to-break-into-scores-of-sites-reuters-exclusive/)
- **AI keys stolen from OpenCode (Oct 16 to 18).** Attackers got into an old part of the OpenCode web console, where some users had saved keys for OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google; 1.4% of console users were affected. A few reported charges above $1,000, one about $1,700 overnight. Anthropic and Google blocked the exposed keys, and OpenAI refunded affected customers. OpenCode's app and command-line tool were not affected. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/hackers-hit-ai-coding-tool-opencode-running-up-charges-on-users-ai-accounts/)
- **LiteLLM, a popular AI building block, poisoned (Oct 21).** For under two hours, from 4:39 to 6:23am PT, version 1.107.0 on PyPI (the main Python package site) stole passwords and keys from any machine that installed it. Some Docker images were bad until 7:20am PT. The attacker used developers' stolen GitHub tokens. The safe version is 1.106.2. If you or a vendor installed 1.107.0, treat every key on that machine as stolen. [LiteLLM](https://docs.litellm.ai/blog/security-incident-oct-21-2026)
- **GitLab fixes a 10 out of 10 flaw (Oct 21).** CVE-2026-110264 lets anyone take over a self-managed GitLab server without logging in. It is the second top-severity GitLab flaw in nine days. GitLab.com is already fixed. [GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/releases/patches/patch-release-gitlab-19-5-1-released/)
- **Home Plex servers hijacked (since Oct 17).** Four chained flaws in Plex Media Server version 1.43.2 and earlier let attackers take over home media servers to mine crypto and relay traffic. A fix (1.43.3.10861) has existed since August, but attacks started a day after details went public on October 16. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) added the flaws to its exploited list on October 20. [Plex](https://forums.plex.tv/t/important-security-update-for-plex-media-server-v1-43-2-and-earlier/942319)
- **A backup tool became the way in (Oct 15 to 19).** Attackers abused ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick), used by many IT providers, to get into organizations' Microsoft 365 email and files. Huntress, a security firm, counted at least 162 compromised Microsoft 365 organizations. Two-step sign-in did not help, because the attacker signed in as the app, not as a person. ConnectWise shut the service off. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-breach-microsoft-365-tenants-via-connectwise-supply-chain-attack/)
- **Rank Math PRO WordPress plugin backdoored (Oct 15 to 16).** Version 3.0.123, downloaded during about an 11-hour window, contained a backdoor. Updating to 3.0.124 does not remove what was planted: restore a backup from before October 15, then change all passwords and keys. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/rank-math-pro-wordpress-plugin-compromised-in-supply-chain-attack/)

## AI and attacks

- Calif found the PHP flaws in August using an OpenAI model, in an audit OpenAI paid for; Wiz found them separately using its own AI tool plus Google and OpenAI models. The same tools help attackers work out an exploit from a published fix, which shrinks the time between "fix released" and "attacks start."
- Fortune reported on October 21 that experts see newer AI models driving a growing wave of attacks, including an early-October breach at South Korean banks where AI appears to have been used. Shinhan Bank alone reported about 25,000 people affected; totals across several institutions are reported at 66,000 to 68,000. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/21/powerful-new-ai-models-are-powering-a-growing-wave-of-cyber-attacks-experts-say/)
- What it means for you: install security updates within days, not weeks; assume scam emails, texts, and calls will be well written and personal; and don't install software someone sends you to join a call, an interview, or a test.

## Poisoned software and stolen keys

- **Malware that punishes you for revoking its stolen token.** Shai-Hulud-style worms spread through code packages, and some versions wipe your home folder if their stolen GitHub token is revoked. They hit 42 TanStack packages in May 2026 and the Tensorlake package on October 8. The published advice is to remove the malware's token monitor first, then revoke tokens, and to treat the machine as compromised. [Socket](https://www.socket.dev/blog/tensorlake-compromise), [MITRE](https://attack.mitre.org/software/S9043/), [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Supply-chain-attack-on-TanStack-42-packages-compromised-11291014.html)
- **GitHub projects robbed of their secrets.** The "GhostAction" campaign used hijacked GitHub accounts to plant code that copies passwords and cloud keys out of projects: 772 repositories in its August to September wave, and more than 30,000 targeted from October 8 to 12, mostly on October 8 and 9, with GitHub blocking it from October 9. [GitGuardian](https://blog.gitguardian.com/ghostaction-github-actions-supply-chain-attack-returns/)
- **LiteLLM was also poisoned in March 2026** (versions 1.82.7 and 1.82.8), by a different route.
- What to do: set spending caps on any AI or cloud account billed by usage; replace keys you've saved in third-party services; keep keys out of code and synced folders; wait about a week on routine package and plugin updates unless they fix an exploited flaw; and when the news names a poisoned version, check whether you have it.

## Hardware wallets and crypto

- **Tampered Ledger wallets from a reseller (reported Oct 9).** Some Ledger wallets sold through CryptoBilis, a reseller in Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines, carried a hidden implant that read the 24-word recovery phrase during setup and sent it out over a cellular connection. Unconfirmed estimates of the losses run from about $72 million to $93 million. Ledger's advice: if you bought from that reseller, don't set the device up, or move funds to a new device with a new phrase. MediaMarktSaturn paused Ledger sales on October 13. [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ledger-finds-unauthorized-hardware.html)
- **Weak Coldcard recovery phrases (since July 30).** A 2021 firmware change made some Coldcard phrases guessable, and thieves have taken about 1,816 bitcoin (about $116 million) since July 30. Updating the firmware does not fix a phrase it already made: create a new phrase on fixed firmware and move the funds. Phrases made with at least 50 fair dice rolls, done in private and never recorded, are not affected. [Coinkite](https://coldcard.com/security/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/03/bitcoin-owners-116-million-hack-coldcard-coinkite-exploit/)
- **Customer lists leaked at wallet makers.** Ledger (via its payment processor, January), Trezor (via its shipping partner, August; Trezor later raised the count to 80,689 customers), and SafePal (August, 39,798 customers). No keys were taken, but expect targeted phishing that knows what you bought. [Trezor](https://trezor.io/blog/news/recent-customer-data-exposed-in-shipping-provider-incident), [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/safepal-data-breach-impacts-39-798-customers-stolen-info-for-sale)
- **Trezor Safe 7 chip attack (June).** Researchers broke one of its chips with a laser in a lab. Trezor says keys aren't stored on that chip and owners need not act. [Trezor](https://trezor.io/blog/news/Trezor-response-TROPIC01-chip-disclosure-no-impact-to-your-funds)
- **LastPass 2022 vaults are still being cracked.** Blockchain analysts have traced more than $35 million in crypto stolen from vaults with weak master passwords, about $7 million of it in September 2025. Treat any recovery phrase stored in LastPass before late 2022 as exposed. [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/trm-traces-stolen-crypto-from-2022-lastpass-breach-on-chain-indicators-suggest-russian-cybercriminal-involvement)
- What to do: buy hardware wallets only from the maker; keep recovery phrases on paper; never type a phrase into a website or app; and move funds whose phrase was ever stored digitally.

## Websites, servers, and remote access

- Small business sites: PHP, Rank Math, and stolen WordPress management keys (an attack on UpdraftCentral keys reported by Patchstack on October 19, from a single source) all landed in the same week. Ask your host which PHP version you run, keep plugins few and current, and keep a backup from before any incident.
- Business remote-access and firewall products on CISA's exploited list in October: Citrix NetScaler (CVE-2026-88779, a crash bug, added October 4), Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935, October 13), Palo Alto firewalls (CVE-2026-0322, October 14), and BeyondTrust Remote Support (CVE-2026-102764, October 16, exploited since August 29). [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
- What to do: if you run a site, a server, or a home media server, find out what it runs, update it, and keep admin pages off the open internet.

## Computers and phones

- **Windows:** Microsoft's October 13 updates fixed a flaw already being exploited (CVE-2026-96951) that removes the warning Windows shows on downloaded files. [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-october-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-1-411-flaws-3-zero-days/)
- **Apple:** Apple's September 28 updates for older systems (iOS and iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1, macOS Sequoia 15.8.1) fixed a flaw used against targeted people on phones older than iOS 27 (CVE-2026-86950). Install whatever update your device offers. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/100100)
- **SIM swaps:** carriers offer free locks. Verizon has SIM Protection and Number Lock, T-Mobile has SIM Protection and Port Out Protection, and AT&T has Wireless Account Lock in the myAT&T app.
- What to do: turn on automatic updates and restart when asked.

## Where to watch

- [CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog): flaws confirmed to be under attack.
- Your vendors' own security pages: Apple, Microsoft, Google, your web host, and any app or plugin you rely on.
- The US National Vulnerability Database now fully analyzes only some flaws. Since April 15, 2026, NIST prioritizes flaws on CISA's exploited list, in government software, or in critical software, so many others wait for NIST's own analysis. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/04/nist-updates-nvd-operations-address-record-cve-growth)
